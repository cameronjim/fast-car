note picks the slam_toolbox loop closure settings on every real oval bag plus the sim regression,
finds where the wheel odometry's yaw error really is, fixes it, and maps the 2026-11-12 room again.
Everything ran offline in the ros-dev image on the dev machine. Nothing ran on the car.

## 1. Map quality metric and the selection rule

A real track has no truth map, so `tools/mapping_eval/quality.py` measures the saved occupancy grid
(`tools/map_quality.py MAP.yaml`):

- `multi_layer_fraction`: rays from free floor that cross two or more separate walls (at least 15 cm
  of free floor between them) before reaching unknown. A wall seen once is one layer; the second
  copy of a doubled wall is a second. The duplicate-wall detector.
- `thickness_p90_m`: width of the occupied band along its ridge (distance transform). Smear.
- wall cells, interior wall cells, free regions (informational).

Checked on known maps before any candidate was scored: the 2026-11-10 oval map 9 percent layers, the
v3 oval map 29, the live doubled 2026-11-12 map 39.

Rule, written before any real-bag replay of the candidates (kept in the scratch directory with the
runs): score B = `multi_layer_fraction + thickness_p90_m` per map. Gate 1: 12 sim seeds (21 to 32)
of the L5 mapping canary with its own thresholds, unchanged, 12 of 12. Gate 2: mean B over the three
real bags at least 0.05 below the repo config's, no bag worse by more than 0.05. Winner: lowest mean B
among configs passing both, else the repo config stays. Candidates: A repo, B slam_toolbox defaults
(chain 10, 0.35 / 0.45), M1 (chain 10, 0.5 / 0.6), M2 (chain 15, 0.35 / 0.45), M3 (chain 15,
0.42 / 0.52, added after the first sim results and before any real-bag map was scored).

**The rule's flaw, plainly.** B adds a fraction to a length in metres, which is arbitrary, and it
underweights the failure that matters: on the first replay (before the yaw fix) the repo config's
map was visibly two rooms and the defaults' map was one thick ring, and B scored them 0.75 and 0.73.
Duplicate rings are therefore reported as a hard failure separately below, and the rule was not
changed after seeing results.

from this change.

- The `_cw` and `_v3` maps are identical under every config: loop closure never fires differently
  there, so those bags cannot choose between the configs.
- No candidate is 0.05 below the repo config, so the repo config stays.
- **Hard failure, separately:** every config fails the full 2026-11-12 bag (34 to 51 percent of rays

  reversing) map cleanly, 7 percent layers; at 1400 scans the second room copy appears. slam_toolbox's
  loop scan matcher searches only +-0.349 rad (`coarse_search_angle_offset`) around the current pose, so
  a heading error beyond about 20 degrees cannot be closed whatever the response thresholds are.

  percent layers against 53 and 44) but worsens `_v3` (36 against 32). Not adopted; worth a decision
  with more bags.

## 2. The wheel yaw rate

### Method

Frame-to-frame ICP (the earlier 0.64 result) reads forward travel low in a featureless corridor
(0.73 of speed times dt). So every scan of a bag is registered against a finished map of the same
run instead (`tools/mapping_eval/register.py`, `tools/yaw_evidence.py`), which gives the true heading
and position with no drift. Over 1 s windows of forward driving the wheel odometry's heading change
and path length are compared with the registered ones, binned by steering angle. Reported as ratios of
sums (the regression slope through the origin agrees to 0.01 at full lock, so timing error is not
diluting it). The 2026-11-10 bags are put on the calibrated scale (speed / 1.401, steering x 1.203
left, x 1.072 right). Maps: `oval.yaml`, `oval_v3.yaml` (2026-11-10 oval), and the v2 map below for
the first lap of 2026-11-12.

### Evidence (true yaw rate / modelled yaw rate, model before this change)

| \|steering\| (rad) | `_cw` left, n | `_v3` right, n | 2026-11-12 lap, n | pooled |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 to 0.15 | 1.084 (271) | 1.102 (331) | 1.501 (49) | 1.12 |
| 0.15 to 0.25 | 0.994 (199) | 0.920 (317) | 1.004 (81) | 0.96 |
| 0.25 to 0.35 | 0.939 (169) | 0.877 (168) | 0.942 (110) | 0.92 |
| 0.35 and up | 0.859 (1216) | 0.858 (1143) | 0.853 (401) | 0.86 |

Ground speed over wheel speed (path length ratio) in the same windows: 0.93 to 1.0, flat in steering
angle (0.92 to 0.97 at full lock on the old bags, 1.00 on the 2026-11-12 lap). So:

- the translation over-read is a uniform 3 to 6 percent, inside the push test's tolerance, and not
  larger in turns. The 0.8 ground over wheel speed at full lock in the dynamics refit (PR 138) is
  most likely ICP's translation bias in turns (the registered path length shows no such drop), not
  the car;
- the curvature part (yaw ratio over speed ratio) is 1.15 at 0.10 rad falling to 0.89 at full lock, the
  steering angle table being a straight line from neutral to each stop where the car's response is
  steeper at small angles and saturates. Full lock left comes out at a rear axle turning radius of
  about 0.65 m, against the measured 0.64 m;
- left and right agree, so one table;
- the earlier ICP figure of 0.64 overstated the error: the registered ratio is 0.86 at full lock and
  about 1.0 at moderate angles.

### Where it is fixed

`steering.yaw_rate_gain_table` in `config/vehicle_params.yaml` (schema 0.16.0, new required field,
every number cited in the field's comment): knots (0.10, 1.12), (0.20, 0.96), (0.30, 0.92), (0.42, 0.86),
piecewise linear in |steering|, held outside. `wheel_twist_node` applies it
(`wz = v g(|delta|) tan(delta) / L`, variance includes the slope). Not folded into
`pwm_to_angle_table` or `min/max_angle_rad`: that would shrink the planning limits every planner and
`racer_safety` use (the right lock would read about 0.41 rad), which is the owner's decision. Wheel speed
is not corrected (a flat 3 to 6 percent; fix with a taped straight and `gear_ratio`, then re-derive the
table, which folds that over-read in; the schema text says so).

The sim's car steers exactly as commanded, so the sim launches (`sim_autopilot.launch.py`, the L5
canaries, the track family runner) pass `yaw_gain_override:=1.0` and are unchanged.

### Heading drift before and after (registered heading against dead reckoned, per radian turned)

| Bag | Turned (rad) | Before | After (table) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-11-11 `_cw` | 89 | 0.146 | 0.047 |
| 2026-11-11 `_v3` | 25 | 0.125 | 0.010 |
| 2026-11-12 first lap | 24 | 0.052 | 0.071 |
| pooled | 138 | 0.126 | 0.044 |

Median heading error at the end of a stretch: `_cw` 0.74 rad to 0.08, `_v3` 0.79 to 0.11. **The
2026-11-12 lap did not improve (1.26 rad to 1.72 rad over 24 rad turned)** although its full-lock
windows agree with the other bags (0.853). Its totals say the unchanged model was already 4 percent
low overall; I could not reconcile that with its windows. The two older bags use the 2026-11-10
calibration conversion of the steering command, which is itself a linear scale; if that conversion is
off, the table is partly fitting it. A real gyro settles this: the VESC IMU poll from PR 142
(`vesc_imu:=true`, `tools/imu_check.py`) is the next measurement, and the table should be refit
against it. Until then treat the table as measured on 138 rad of turning, good on two bags and
neutral to slightly worse on the third.
