# Residual policy v1 and the on-car policy node, 2026-11-11

**Sim only. Zero-shot. Not the thesis result.** Phase 3 system ID has not run, so the simulator is
still unfitted (the tyre is a unit-correct placeholder, actuator lags and delays are guesses) and
nothing in this note was measured on the car. `policy_node` here is roadmap 5.2 scaffolding: it
does NOT deploy on hardware and does NOT pass gate G3 (the classical baseline has not driven the
real track reliably yet). Roadmap 5.1, 5.2 and 5.4 stay open.

## Result in one paragraph

**The v1 residual does not beat the tracker in sim. It is slightly slower and no safer.** Trained
for 1.5M steps in the corrected sim, three seeds per configuration, the chosen policy changes
paired lap time by +0.14% to +0.40% (slower) across the three seeds and the four held-out
conditions (CIs exclude zero, so it is consistently a hair slower, not noise), with identical
completion (115/120, 119/120, 120/120; 95 against 97 of 120 when every range is widened 1.5x, two
more crashes). Not one of the 72 checkpoints scored on the validation pool beat the tracker.
The v0 result (about 1% faster) did not reproduce on a re-tuned base in the corrected sim; I did
not test why, but the diagnosis below says there is almost no headroom for it to find: the
re-tuned base already runs at the envelope's 2.0 m/s speed cap for 87% of all steps, and a policy
that blindly asks for the full +0.1 m/s gets only 0.4% (a trivial reference, below). So: no win,
a valid result, and a deployment path (contract, node, launch, app) that is ready for whichever
policy comes next. Nothing here says anything about the real car.

## Training setup, budget and selection

**Base tracker re-tuned first** (fair baseline), `racer_train.tune_base` under the full
randomisation on tracks the car can drive, tuning pool seeds 3000+ and episode seeds 60000+
(disjoint from training 1000+, validation 5000+ and the held-out pools): pass 1, 216 settings x 48
two-lap episodes (lookahead min 0.6 to 1.0, max 1.0 to 1.6, curvature reference 0.6 to 1.5, `a_lat`
2.5 to 4.0, top speed 1.8 and 2.0), 10,368 episodes, 159 s on 8 workers; pass 2 around the winner,
40 settings x 96 episodes, 3,840 episodes, 52 s. Total 14,208 episodes, 0.47 CPU hours. Chosen
(best score, mean speed with a miss counting zero, completion floor 90%): lookahead 1.0 to 1.2 m,
curvature reference 1.0 1/m, `a_lat` 4.0 m/s2, top speed 2.0 m/s, 99% completion and 1.88 m/s
on completed laps on the tuning pool. Settings within about 1% of that score (for example 3.5
m/s2, 100% completion) are inside the noise. This base is strong: 95.8% on the held-out family
draws (115/120), 99.2% on unseen shapes, 100% nominal. The five held-out family failures are wall
contacts on ordinary tracks (inner radius 0.31 to 0.72 m, both directions) under hard draws, not
thin islands. `a_lat` 4.0 is a sim-tuned number against a friction range of 0.55 to 1.1; the real
car's Phase 4 tuning replaces it.

**Training.** SAC (stable-baselines3), 128x128 MLP, gamma 0.99, buffer 1M, reward unchanged from
v0 (progress, crash weight 50, envelope violation 0.1), frozen observation statistics baked into
`policy.pt`. Two configurations, 3 seeds each, 1.5M steps each: `sac_residual_v1` (quarter
authority, fractions 0.025 / 0.004, v0's best) and `sac_residual_v1_eighth` (0.0125 / 0.002, a
dose-response check on the knob v0 found to matter). 9.0M steps in all, 56 minutes of wall
clock with six runs at once (about 430 to 480 steps per second each, while the Docker builds and
tests below ran on the same machine). Every other setting is v0's, including the randomisation
(only the stiffness range changed, on the tyre note's recommendation). Configs:
`training/configs/v1/`. The two arms differ only in the residual: both go through the same
envelope and the same 2.0 m/s cap.

**Selection, validation only.** All 72 checkpoints (12 per run, every 125k steps) were scored on
the validation pool (16 tracks, seeds 5000+, episode seeds 70000+) with 64 paired two-lap episodes
each. The rule, fixed before looking: per configuration each seed's best checkpoint; the
configuration with the higher mean of those; within it the best seed. Result: **zero of 72
checkpoints beat the tracker** (best -0.0004 in the units of mean speed with a miss counting
zero). Median checkpoint: -0.061 (quarter) and -0.032 (eighth), completion 95.3% and 96.9%
against the tracker's 98.4%, lap time 0.86% and 0.35% slower. Per-seed bests: quarter -0.009,
-0.032, -0.027 (mean -0.0225); eighth -0.026, -0.0004, -0.002 (mean -0.0095). So the eighth
configuration (the one closest to doing nothing) is chosen, and its best seed is `eighth_s2`,
checkpoint 875,000. Winner's curse applies (best of 72 on 64 episodes); the held-out numbers
below are what to read. `validation_selection.json` in the policy pointer directory has all 72
rows.

**Where the headroom went (diagnostics, not a fix).**

| Reference | family, completion | paired lap time |
|---|---|---|
| zero action through the same pairing | 115 / 115 | 0.000% (identical; the pairing is exact) |
| constant "always +0.1 m/s, steering untouched" | 115 / 115 (same 5 crashes) | -0.41% (CI -0.51 to -0.31) |

(`racer_train.reference_policies`.) The chosen policy's mean signed speed residual over 17,284
steps is +0.011 m/s (the bound is 0.10), its mean absolute steering residual 0.0064 rad (bound
0.0224). The re-tuned base is at the envelope's 2.0 m/s cap on 87% of steps, where a positive
speed residual does nothing (the cap clips it silently, and that is not counted as a clip), so
the speed channel is dead weight on this base: the raceline's top speed equals the cap. What is
left is steering in the 13% of steps below the cap, and in this sim the learned steering residual
does not find anything there. A design note for whoever continues: with the cap at the raceline's
top speed the speed channel cannot contribute on straights; the base top speed would have to sit
at least the speed authority below the cap, which makes the baseline a deliberately slower
strawman, so that is a human decision, not something done here. Not tried, and not claimed:
longer training, larger networks, a different algorithm, reward weights (all v0's pilots said
less authority and a bigger crash weight help; this run already has both).

## The policy node

```
tracker_node --(/tracker/command)--> policy_node --(/drive_raw)--> safety_node --> /drive --> vesc_node
                                       |-- /policy/status (1 Hz JSON), /policy/events (JSON)
```

**Interface choice: a handoff topic.** `tracker_node` gained one parameter, `drive_topic`
(default `/drive_raw`, so nothing changes for anyone else). `policy.launch.py` runs it with
`drive_topic:=/tracker/command`; `policy_node` subscribes and is then the only publisher of
`/drive_raw`. The alternatives were worse: two publishers on `/drive_raw` interleave at the safety
node (it would see a 50 Hz tracker stream and a 50 Hz policy stream, and its rate limits and
watchdog are written for one), and a mux node is one more thing on the command path. The tracker
still computes its command exactly as before, `safety_node` and the hardware mux are untouched,
and `policy_node` never publishes `/drive`. If `policy_node` dies, `/drive_raw` goes quiet and
the safety watchdog brakes; `policy.launch.py` shuts the whole launch down when `policy_node`
exits, so a refused policy never leaves a tracker running unobserved or falls back to driving by
itself. Tests: `test_tracker_node_drive_topic_launch.py` (the handoff tracker publishes only
there, the default tracker only on `/drive_raw`), `test_policy_launch.py`, and the canary checks
the publisher counts (`/drive_raw` 1, `/drive` 1, `/tracker/command` 1).

**One decision per tracker command.** Driven by that message, no second timer, so there is no
extra scheduling hop. In order: freshness (no fresh `/odom` means no output at all, the tracker is
on the same watchdog), the pose the tracker also follows (`/odom`, composed with TF
`pose_frame -> odom` when `pose_frame` is set, exactly as `tracker_node` does it; on the car the
localiser's `/pose` reaches both nodes through that TF, so the observation and the tracker see one
pose), the observation, the network, the scaled residual, `envelope.apply` (the training
library, not a copy), and a float32-safe clamp on publication.

**Observation, as in training.** `racer_policy.live_observation` is the deploy-side twin of
`racer_train.observation` and `ResidualRacerEnv._sense`: 11 low-dimensional fields (speed and yaw
rate from `/odom` twist, the kinematic slip proxy from the previous enveloped command, lateral and
heading error and the two curvature previews from the raceline CSV the tracker also loads, the
tracker's command, the previous enveloped command) then 36 LiDAR beams. The scan is the one real
difference of geometry: training sees 1080 beams over 4.7 rad min-pooled by 30, the car's RPLidar
is 360 degrees with its head mounted at `sensors.lidar.mount_yaw_rad` (pi). `pool_scan` bins every
live beam by its vehicle bearing into the same 36 bins of 4.7/36 rad and takes the minimum, which
is the same operation (identical output on a training-geometry scan), clips to 5 m, and reads
NaN, infinity and anything under the sensor's minimum range as no return. The live scan is simply
held between sweeps (about 10 Hz), which is what training's 4 to 6 step refresh simulates.

**Fallback to the plain tracker command**, with the residual forced to zero (the command still goes
through the envelope's rate limits and speed cap), when: the envelope's OOD scorer fires; no scan
yet or the last one is older than `scan_timeout_s` (0.5 s); the network raises or returns a
non-finite or misshapen action. Each entry and exit is one JSON record on `/policy/events` and one
log line (like the safety node: engage once, release once with a duration); per-step flags are
counted in `/policy/status`.

**Published status.** `/policy/status` (std_msgs/String, JSON, 1 Hz): state, reason, counters
(steps, fallback steps by reason, residual clipped, rate limited, OOD, residual magnitude over the
steps it was applied) and two fixed-bin histograms with p50, p99 and max: inference time
(50 us to 20 ms) and loop period (10 to 100 ms, nominal 20). `/tracker/command`, `/policy/status`
and `/policy/events` are in the bag regex of `car_teleop.launch.py` (invariant 5).

**Refusals (invariant 3), one function for the node and the app.** `racer_policy.deploy.
load_deployment` runs, in order: `load_contract` (manifest schema, `contract_version` major,
`policy.pt` checksum), `verify_against_environment` against the LIVE `vehicle_params` and the live
observation schema this build produces (version, `sysid_session_id`, field names, order, dtypes,
units, LiDAR block), and the checks the contract schema cannot hold. The contract is
`additionalProperties: false`, so everything else lives in `policy_meta.yaml`, which the loader
does not read; v0 left its `must_match_at_deploy` section to "the deploy node must check", and
this is that check. A directory without a consistent sidecar is refused: residual fractions and
action scales must equal the contract's, control rate 50 Hz, observation profile and LiDAR block
must be what this build produces, the contract's normalisation must be the identity (the
statistics are baked into `policy.pt`, a non-identity block would be applied by nobody),
`vehicle_overrides_assumed` must be empty (a policy trained on numbers the live params do not
contain is a policy for another car whatever the version says), the recorded actuator rate limits
must equal the live ones, and the speed cap cannot exceed the vehicle's. The envelope settings
(fractions, speed cap, OOD reference and threshold) are read from the policy directory, never from
node parameters, and `policy.launch.py` starts the tracker with the lookahead the policy trained
on. There is no parameter that skips a check (a test passes `ignore_mismatch:=true` and similar and
shows the refusal stands). The node exits 2 with `REFUSING TO START: <ExceptionName>: <why>`
before subscribing to anything, and also refuses a model that fails to load or that rejects a
dry 47-wide observation or returns the wrong shape.

## What the policy node does not do yet

- It has not run on the Jetson. The inference figures below are from a Mac, in a Linux VM, with
  the training run competing for the CPU.
- It has not driven a real track. The tracker has not (the localised tracker is being debugged on
  another branch), and G3 is not passed.
- The OOD scorer is the reference normalised-distance scorer from `training/envelope`, not a
  critic-disagreement detector.
- `torch` must be in the car image (the optional Jetson torch layer in `docker/car`); the node
  refuses to start without it. The CI job installs the CPU wheel.

## What remains before an on-car trial

1. The tracker has to drive the real track on the localised pose, reliably, alone (G3's own
   precondition; the localised tracker is being debugged on `fix/localised-tracker`). Nothing
   here moves that.
2. The base and the policy have to agree. The car's tracker is tuned for 0.64 m/s with a 0.35 to
   0.7 m lookahead; v1's base is 2 m/s with 1.0 to 1.2 m, and in the bridge sim the tracker alone
   does not lap at 0.64 m/s with the policy's lookahead. Either the car's tracker runs the policy's
   base at a speed it can hold, or the policy is retrained on the car's real base and speed (the
   randomisation, the track family and the base tuning are all parameters of `training/configs`).
   A learned residual with no demonstrated sim advantage on its own base is a weak reason to do
   the first.
3. Phase 3 system ID (a fitted tyre, actuator lags, delays), then retrain: roadmap 5.1 stays open,
   and this v1 is superseded by it.
4. torch in the car image and the Jetson latency and jitter numbers (`racer_train.latency` on the
   Jetson, then `/policy/status` on a run); port `policy_node` to C++ only if the tail is bad.
5. A decision about the speed channel (the cap equals the raceline's top speed; see the diagnostics).
6. Then the runbook below, wheels off the ground first.

## First on-car trial: runbook (do not start before the preconditions hold)

This is a zero-shot, report-only measurement (roadmap 5.2), not an evaluation. It follows the
standing rules in `first-boot-runbook.md` ("Before you start"): two people, the driver's hand on
the RC kill knob for every run, wheels off the ground first, every run bagged with rail voltage.

**Preconditions. Any "no" means stop.**

1. The tracker alone laps the real track reliably on the localised pose (G3's own precondition),
   and its baseline laps are recorded. Until then the policy has nothing to be compared with.
2. The car's checkout is on `vehicle_params` 0.14.0 or whatever the policy's contract names (the
   check below says which), and `docker exec car-stack python3 -c "import torch"` works (the
   optional Jetson torch layer in `docker/car`).
3. Copy the policy onto the car and check it with the node's own checks (the app does the same):
   ```
   mkdir -p data/policies && cp -r <car-data>/policies/v1-2026-11-11 data/policies/
   docker run --rm -v $PWD:/workspace -w /workspace -e PYTHONPATH=/workspace/ros_ws/src/racer_policy/src:/workspace/ros_ws/src/racer_tools:/workspace/training/envelope/src \
     --entrypoint python3 <car image> -m racer_policy.check data/policies/v1-2026-11-11     # prints OK
   uv run python -m racer_train.latency --policy-dir data/policies/v1-2026-11-11           # the Jetson number
   ```
   Anything but `OK` stops here. Do not edit a yaml to get past a refusal; re-exporting against
   the live parameters is a human decision.
4. **The base the policy trained on must be one the tracker alone can drive on this track.** The
   policy rides on lookahead 1.0 to 1.2 m, curvature reference 1.0 and a raceline tuned for
   2 m/s (`must_match_at_deploy.base_controller` in its sidecar). In the sim bridge, at 0.64 m/s on
   a 1 m stadium, the tracker alone with that lookahead does not lap (it stalls on safety brakes in
   both directions); the car's current tracker tuning is 0.35 to 0.7 m at 0.64 m/s. Run arm A
   below, alone, on the real track first. If it does not lap with the policy's lookahead at the
   speed you can run, STOP: there is nothing to compare and the policy is out of its operating
   range; the fix is a v2 trained on the car's actual base and speed, not a changed parameter.
5. A raceline for the real map from the track-session tool, as slow as the tracker needs. The
   policy trained at 0 to 2 m/s with the OOD reference speed at 1.8 m/s (scale 0.8), so at 0.64 m/s
   the speed term alone contributes 1.45 of the 3.0 threshold and the node may fall back often.
   That is expected and is data (the fallback fraction is in `/policy/status`), not a failure;
   it also means a slow first run measures plumbing, not benefit.

**Stage 1, wheels off the ground, stack up with localisation and the recorder.**

1. Start from the app (ML policy, with the raceline chosen) or by hand:
   `ros2 launch racer_bringup policy.launch.py policy_dir:=/workspace/data/policies/v1-2026-11-11 raceline:=<csv>`.
   The launch dies at once with `REFUSING TO START: ...` if the policy does not match this car.
2. `ros2 topic echo /policy/status --once`: `state: active`, `counters.steps` climbing at 50 per
   second, `loop_period_ms` p50 near 20, `inference_us` p99 under 20,000 (one period; report the
   real p99, and if the tail is bad this is the moment the node gets ported to C++).
3. `ros2 topic info /drive_raw` must show ONE publisher (policy_node) and `/drive` one (the safety
   node). If the tracker is also on `/drive_raw`, stop: the handoff is not in effect.
4. With the wheels spinning, compare `/tracker/command` with `/drive_raw` in the bag: the
   difference must stay within +-0.0224 rad of steering and +-0.10 m/s of speed, and the speed
   never above 2.0 m/s.
5. Demonstrate the fallback (roadmap 5.4): (a) stop the LiDAR driver: `/policy/events` shows one
   `fallback_engage` with reason `scan_stale` within 0.5 s, `/drive_raw` equals `/tracker/command`,
   and one `fallback_release` when the scan returns; (b) publish an `/initialpose` about 1.5 m off
   the line: reason `ood`, same equality. Record both.
6. RC knob kill and STOP ALL, once each, with the policy running.
